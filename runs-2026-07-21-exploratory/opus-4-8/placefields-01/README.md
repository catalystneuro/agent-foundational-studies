# Hippocampal place cells in DANDI:000044

## Dataset

[DANDI:000044](https://dandiarchive.org/dandiset/000044), *"Diversity in neural firing dynamics
supports both rigid and learned hippocampal sequences"* (Grosmark & Buzsáki, *Science* 2016; the
`hc-11` dataset). Eight sessions from four Long-Evans rats, each with bilateral silicon-probe
recordings from dorsal CA1, spike-sorted units labelled excitatory or inhibitory, 128-channel LFP,
and video tracking on a maze, organised into a PRE sleep epoch, a maze-running epoch, and a POST
sleep epoch. Files are 5-9 GB each and were never downloaded in full: `remfile` plus `h5py` served
HDF5 chunk reads from S3 with a local disk cache, so only the units table, the behaviour module, and
short windows of LFP were transferred.

Five of the eight sessions were recorded on a linear track (four at 1.6 m, one at 2 m) and are
analysed here. The other three used a closed circular maze, where a lap wraps around and the
two-direction traversal logic used throughout does not apply; they were excluded on maze geometry
before any spiking data were examined.

## What was analysed

The linearized position was segmented into individual track traversals, split by travel direction,
and masked by a 5 cm/s running-speed threshold. For each putative pyramidal cell and each direction,
the analysis computed an occupancy-normalised rate map, Skaggs spatial information, a split-half
(odd versus even traversal) reliability correlation, and a null distribution of spatial information
generated by circularly shifting each spike train on a time axis in which the traversals are
concatenated. That null preserves each train's own burstiness, refractoriness, and mean rate while
destroying its alignment to position, so it is considerably stricter than shuffling spike times
outright. A cell counts as a place cell in a given direction if its information exceeds the 99th
percentile of its own null, its peak rate is at least 1 Hz, and its odd/even map correlation exceeds
0.5. Position was then decoded from held-out traversals with `pynapple.decode_bayes`, and a Poisson
GLM with a 12-function B-spline basis over position was fitted per cell with NeMoS and scored on
held-out traversals with McFadden's pseudo-R². All computation used Pynapple data structures.

Two defects in the published NWB files had to be handled explicitly, and neither is detectable
without checking. The `SpatialSeries.rate` field holds the sampling period (0.0256 s) rather than
the frequency, so taking it at face value spreads 80,762 position samples over 36 days; the time
base is rebuilt as `starting_time + i * rate` and asserted against the MazeEpoch duration. Separately,
in three sessions the units-table row with id 2 contains two full-session spike trains concatenated
end to end, so its spike times jump backwards part way through. Pynapple sorts such a train silently,
which would turn it into a plausible-looking but meaningless unit, so the check is done on the raw
ragged array and the offending row is dropped.

## Key finding

The dataset reproduces every defining property of the hippocampal place code. Pooled across the five
linear-track sessions, 211 of 270 putative CA1 pyramidal cells (78 %) qualified as place cells in at
least one travel direction, yielding 329 place fields with a median width of 32 cm at half maximum,
a median in-field peak rate of 6.0 Hz, and a median spatial information of 0.61 bits per spike.
Fields tiled the whole track, and sorting cells by their rightward field peak scrambled the leftward
rate maps, so the code represents position together with travel direction rather than position
alone. The effect is already visible in the raw spike raster: with units ordered by the location of
their rightward field peak, the population sweeps once through that ordering as a clear diagonal on
each rightward traversal.

The strongest evidence is that the code generalises. Fitting tuning curves on half the traversals
and decoding the other half, without selecting for place cells, recovered the animal's position to a
median error of 5.8 to 14.1 cm across sessions, against 43 to 65 cm when each unit's tuning curve was
circularly rolled by a random amount. Decoding accuracy improved monotonically with the number of
cells used, as a distributed population code predicts. Independently, in the prototype session the
Poisson GLM gave a held-out pseudo-R² above 0.01 for 67 of 71 rightward place cells (94 %) and 64 of
71 leftward place cells (90 %), but for only 6 of 34 (18 %) and 2 of 34 (6 %) of the remaining
pyramidal cells. Its ranking of cells agreed with the Skaggs information, so two methodologically
unrelated criteria select the same population.
