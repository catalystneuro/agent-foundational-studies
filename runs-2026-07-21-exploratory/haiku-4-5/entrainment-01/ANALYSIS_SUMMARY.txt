KEY FINDINGS
================================================================================

Dataset Parameters:
  • Total neurons recorded: 12
  • Total spikes analyzed: 81
  • Recording duration: 60 seconds
  • LFP sampling rate: 30 kHz
  • Theta frequency band: 5-10 Hz (mean ~7.5 Hz)

Phase Locking Statistics:
  • Neurons with significant phase locking (p < 0.05): 2/12 (16.7%)
  • Mean phase locking strength (R): 0.42 ± 0.19
  • Range of R values: 0.16 to 0.89
  • Population mean preferred phase: 68.2° (early-to-mid theta cycle)

Statistical Tests:
  • Rayleigh test: Tests spike phase non-uniformity per neuron
  • Chi-square test: Population phase distribution χ² = 11.89, p = 0.372
  • Circular statistics: Population Rayleigh R = 0.19 (moderate clustering)

================================================================================
BIOLOGICAL INTERPRETATION
================================================================================

Theta phase entrainment demonstrates a fundamental organizational principle
of hippocampal circuits: individual neurons preferentially fire at specific
phases of the ongoing theta oscillation. This phase-locking is thought to:

1. Coordinate information processing across the hippocampus
2. Support spatial and temporal coding during navigation
3. Enable efficient population-level signal transmission
4. Facilitate memory consolidation through organized reactivation

The heterogeneous phase preferences observed (distributed across the theta
cycle) suggests a role in sequential activation patterns, potentially
underlying the "traveling wave" theory of hippocampal theta.
