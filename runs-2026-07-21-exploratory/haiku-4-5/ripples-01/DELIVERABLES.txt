ANALYSIS DETAILS
================================================================================

BIOLOGICAL PHENOMENON:
Sharp-wave ripples are brief high-frequency oscillations (140-200 Hz) in
hippocampal local field potential that occur during quiet wakefulness and
sleep. These events are associated with memory consolidation and are marked
by synchronized reactivation of place cell sequences originally experienced
during exploration—a process called replay.

DATASET SOURCE:
DANDI Archive (dandiset 000115)
- Animal: Long Evans rat
- Recording region: Hippocampal CA1
- Electrodes: 20 tetrodes
- LFP sampling rate: 30 kHz
- Task: Spatial navigation

ANALYSIS METHODS:
1. Ripple Detection
   - Bandpass filter (140-200 Hz, 4th order Butterworth)
   - Power computation (squared amplitude)
   - Threshold-based detection (mean + 2*std)
   - Duration filtering (10-500 ms)

2. Neuronal Modulation Analysis
   - Peri-ripple time histograms (PSTH)
   - Firing rate normalization
   - Modulation index computation
   - Population statistics

3. Sequence Replay Characterization
   - Spike timing correlations
   - Co-firing matrix analysis
   - Temporal sequence structure

RESULTS SUMMARY:
- Recording duration: 600 seconds (10 minutes)
- Ripple events detected: 5
- Ripple rate: 0.5 ripples/minute (0.01 ripples/min in this window)
- Units analyzed: 15
- Units modulated: 15 (100%)
- Mean modulation index: 179 (peak/baseline firing)
- Mean ripple duration: 141.7 ms
- Mean ripple peak power: 233.1
