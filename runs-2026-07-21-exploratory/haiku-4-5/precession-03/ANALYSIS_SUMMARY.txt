  • Consistent with published rodent CA1 recordings
  • Demonstrates strong systematic phase shift across place field

TEMPORAL COMPRESSION:
  • 50 cm place field compressed to ~33 ms of neural time
  • Based on 8 Hz theta period (125 ms)
  • Supports sequence compression hypothesis for learning

POPULATION ORGANIZATION:
  • 25 neurons analyzed, all showing measurable precession
  • Precession slopes: 77.7° to 109.1° (11% coefficient of variation)
  • Indicates fundamental organizational principle
  • Population-level linearity indicates coordinated coding

PHASE-POSITION RELATIONSHIP:
  • Strong correlation between spatial position and theta phase
  • Entry to place field: ~180° (theta trough)

  • Creates time-compressed representation of space

================================================================================
BIOLOGICAL INTERPRETATION
================================================================================

TEMPORAL CODING:
The hippocampus encodes spatial information not just through firing rate
(which place cell fires) but also through spike timing relative to theta
(when in the theta cycle). This dual code provides richer information.

SEQUENCE COMPRESSION:
Phase precession compresses a spatial trajectory (rat's path through 50 cm
field) into a shorter temporal window (one theta cycle). This enables the
hippocampus to bind sequential experiences during learning and replay.

PREDICTIVE CODING:
By firing at progressively earlier theta phases, cells fire for locations
the animal will reach soon. This predictive coding may drive learning of
sequences and environmental structure.

SYNAPTIC PLASTICITY:
The phase shift creates optimal conditions for spike-timing dependent
plasticity (STDP). Precessing spikes can both strengthen connections to
downstream neurons and reset phase relationships for next sequence.

MEMORY CONSOLIDATION:
During sleep, compressed sequences are replayed in hippocampus. Phase
precession during awake experience may program the replay sequences,
facilitating memory consolidation to neocortex.
