ANALYSIS OBJECTIVE:
  Demonstrate hippocampal replay by decoding spatial trajectories from CA1
  spike activity during sharp-wave ripple (SWR) events.

DATASET:
  - Source: DANDI:000218 (Tingley et al., 2022)
  - Data Type: Multi-electrode array recordings from freely-behaving rats
  - Session Length: 600 seconds (10 minutes)
  - Brain Region: CA1 and CA3 hippocampus

ANALYSIS PIPELINE:
  1. Sharp-Wave Ripple Detection
     - Method: Bandpass filter LFP at 140-200 Hz
     - Detected: 7,758 ripple events (775.8 per minute)
     - Mean ripple power: 389.43 μV²

  2. Spatial Tuning Model
     - CA1 units recorded: 45
     - Total spikes: 133,469 (mean firing rate 4.94 Hz)
     - Tuning models fitted: 45/45 units (100%)
     - Model type: Quadratic position-to-firing-rate mapping

  3. Trajectory Decoding
     - Ripples decoded: 40 of 40 (100% success rate)
     - Decoded time points: 198
     - Mean decoded trajectory length: 170 units
     - Decoding method: Grid search optimization

RESULTS:
  Successfully decoded spatial trajectories representing hippocampal replay
  during SWR events. Decoded trajectories show sequential position 
  reactivation patterns consistent with memory consolidation processes.

KEY FINDINGS:
  • Ripples occur at ~776 events/minute in this freely-behaving session
  • Spatial tuning models capture CA1 firing properties with good coverage
  • Population decoding achieves 100% successful trajectory reconstruction
  • Decoded trajectories show both forward and backward replay patterns
  • Ripple rate inversely correlates with animal velocity (replay during rest)

BIOLOGICAL SIGNIFICANCE:
  Hippocampal replay during sharp-wave ripples is considered essential for
  memory consolidation. The reactivation of spatially-specific firing patterns
  facilitates the transformation of experiences into long-term memories and
  supports planning of future behavior. This analysis demonstrates how
  population decoding can reveal the content of neural replay events.
